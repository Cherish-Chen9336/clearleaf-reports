# Clearleaf privacy policy

Last updated: November 14, 2026

Clearleaf is an ad blocker for Chrome, based on uBlock Origin Lite. This
page explains what it does with your data. In short: **Clearleaf doesn't
collect, sell or send your data anywhere.**

## What stays on your computer

Clearleaf saves these in your browser's extension storage, on your
computer only:

- your settings, such as the blocking level for each site;
- sites you paused or turned off, and when a pause ends;
- filter lists you added yourself, and your own filters.

They aren't synced to your Google account or sent to anyone. Removing
Clearleaf deletes them. Settings → Backup saves them to a file only when
you ask.

## What Clearleaf reads

To block ads and trackers, Clearleaf works inside the pages you visit and
checks the web addresses they load. This happens in your browser. Clearleaf
doesn't record your browsing history and doesn't send page addresses or
page content anywhere.

The "blocked on this page" number comes from Chrome, for the tab you are
looking at, when you open the Clearleaf popup. It isn't saved.

## When Clearleaf connects to the internet

- **Filter lists you add yourself.** If you add a filter list by its web
  address, Clearleaf downloads it from that address, and again later to
  keep it up to date. The built-in lists come with the extension and are
  updated when the extension is updated.
- **Reports, only when you click.** "Send a report" and "Find similar
  reports" open GitHub in a new tab. The report form is filled in with the
  page address you choose, the kind of problem, and Clearleaf's settings
  (filter lists and blocking levels). Nothing is sent until you submit the
  form on GitHub, with your own GitHub account, where the report is
  public. GitHub's own privacy policy applies there.

Clearleaf has no analytics, no tracking and no ads, and it doesn't share
data with anyone.
